Topic 03 — Motor Accessibility Keyboard Flow & Target Size

Motor Accessibility: Precision, Fatigue, and Control

Not everyone navigates digital interfaces using a smooth mouse or agile touchscreen gestures. Millions navigate using keyboards, mouth wands, switch devices, or hands affected by tremors, arthritis, and fatigue.

01 The Lived Barrier

When Pixel Precision Demands Impossible Effort

Motor limitations include conditions such as Parkinson's disease, cerebral palsy, muscular dystrophy, repetitive strain injury (RSI), stroke recovery, and temporary arm injuries. Many people cannot hold a mouse steady or make precise 10-pixel adjustments on a screen.

When developers create microscopic click targets (under 24px) or delete focus indicators (outline: none), keyboard-only navigators and individuals with tremors face constant failure. A minor tremor triggers repeated misclicks, while keyboard navigators lose track of where they are on the page. Simple daily interactions become physically exhausting and frustrating.

03 Connecting to Everyday Life

Physical Exhaustion and Lost Independence

Navigating with a keyboard or assistive switch device requires physical stamina. When buttons lack visible focus rings, users must repeatedly press Tab while guessing where their cursor landed. If the tab sequence jumps erratically across the page, a simple two-minute form can turn into twenty minutes of frustrating trial and error.

For people experiencing muscle fatigue, motor tremors, or joint pain, poorly sized targets and confusing keyboard paths do not just cause inconvenience—they cause genuine physical discomfort and force individuals to abandon tasks they could otherwise complete independently.

04 Practical Solutions

How Designers and Developers Remove This Barrier

  • •
    Ensure high-visibility focus indicators: Never remove focus outlines (outline: none) without providing a bold, high-contrast :focus-visible indicator.
  • •
    Adhere to minimum target sizes: Ensure all interactive buttons, links, and form fields have a minimum touch target size of at least 44×44 pixels (WCAG 2.5.5).
  • •
    Preserve logical source order: Rely on natural DOM HTML order for keyboard navigation instead of unpredictable positive tabindex values.
  • •
    Support full keyboard functionality: Ensure every action that can be performed with a mouse can also be triggered using standard keyboard keys (Tab, Enter, Space, and arrow keys).
