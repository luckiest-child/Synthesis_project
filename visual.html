Topic 01 — Visual Accessibility Perception & Text Equivalents

Visual Accessibility: Beyond the Visible Surface

Millions of people browse the web using screen readers, screen magnifiers, or customized color schemes. When vital instructions or graphics lack non-visual equivalents, everyday tasks become insurmountable obstacles.

01 The Lived Barrier

When Critical Information Is Locked in Unannounced Pixels

Vision impairments vary widely. A person may experience total blindness, low vision, macular degeneration, tunnel vision, or color vision deficiency. Many rely on assistive technologies like screen readers, which convert digital text into synthetic speech or refreshable braille.

When a web application presents important information solely through a graphic—such as a medication label, an order confirmation chart, or a system diagram—without providing text equivalents or accessible semantics, users who cannot see the screen receive nothing. They are forced to rely on others for basic privacy, healthcare decisions, and financial management.

03 Connecting to Everyday Life

The Cost of Inaccessible Visual Information

When essential information exists only as an image or an unlabelled visual element, people who cannot see the screen face serious risks. In high-stakes contexts like healthcare, banking, or travel, having to guess an instruction is dangerous.

Beyond the immediate task failure, this design barrier forces individuals into unnecessary dependence on family members or strangers. True web accessibility preserves personal dignity and self-sufficiency by ensuring that every visual piece of information has a complete, programmatic text alternative.

04 Practical Solutions

How Designers and Developers Remove This Barrier

  • •
    Provide informative alternative text: Ensure all meaningful images have an alt attribute describing their actual content and function, not raw filenames.
  • •
    Render critical information as real text: Never embed essential instructions, numbers, or dosages solely inside flattened image files.
  • •
    Maintain sufficient color contrast: Ensure text meets the WCAG AA minimum ratio of 4.5:1 against its background so visitors with low vision can read comfortably.
  • •
    Support text scaling: Allow users to resize text up to 200% without breaking layouts, clipping words, or requiring horizontal scrolling.
